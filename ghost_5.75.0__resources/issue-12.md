Business models
---------------

![](https://ghost.org/resources/content/images/2019/02/vwsn3jkilmyseozz2xtf.png)
### [What am I worth to advertisers? My obsessive quest to put a price on my attention](https://gizmodo.com/what-am-i-worth-to-advertisers-my-obsessive-quest-to-p-1828343202?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

This Gizmodo writer wanted to find out what the advertisements they were subjected to all day, and even on single pages, were worth. There’s a price to attention, after all, but most of us don’t see it. Their journey to discover this, and the actual number itself, are fairly surprising. A good reason for subscription publishers to feel great about what they’re doing, too.

### [Anchor now lets podcasters receive Patreon-style pledges from listeners](https://www.theverge.com/2018/8/9/17660242/anchor-listener-support-pledge-feature?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

Listener Support allows listeners to pledge $1, $5, or $10 per month to specific podcasts. Each podcast creator will have to enable the feature, and it follows in the footsteps of both Google Podcasts and Overcast’s apps experimenting with the same. We’re looking forward to seeing just how much listeners love being subjected to fewer Mailchimp ads.

### [Why readers pay for news (And why they don't)](https://blog.disqus.com/why-readers-pay-for-news?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

Interesting research from Disqus, the company best known for running a comment system seen on many publisher websites. With 1,200 respondents, about 30 percent had paid for news in the last year, a higher number than expected.

### [Two latin American paywall success stories](https://blog.wan-ifra.org/2018/08/14/two-latin-american-paywall-success-stories?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

While newspaper paywalls remain relatively rare in Latin America, two papers there have achieved success with paid-content strategies. It’s interesting to read about what succeeds in markets beyond the US, and how these publishers have gone about building their subscriber base.

Good reads
----------

![](https://ghost.org/resources/content/images/2019/02/facebook-2.jpg)
### [Twitter and Facebook are platforms, not publishers](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/08/the-messy-democratizing-beauty-of-the-internet/567194/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

A fantastic take on the state of media, and how people are finally coming around to realise that Facebook, Twitter and others shouldn’t be the gatekeepers of publishers. As we’ve found out the hard way, they’re flippant and sometimes lazy! Worse still, their goals don’t actually align with that of the media, making for an awkward uneven balance.

### [How Snap Is Becoming Twitter](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/how-snap-is-becoming-twitter?shared=371b7bab7050abe7&utm_campaign=Revue%20newsletter&utm_medium=Newsletter&utm_source=The%20Interface)

A few years ago, when Snap was a fast-growing, hot commodity venerated by venture capitalists, some would occasionally ask whether it would end up like Twitter—a niche platform with dedicated users but without the broad scale of, say, Facebook. It’s now becoming clear the answer is yes.

### [New WordPress policy allows it to shut down blogs for 'malicious' content](https://techcrunch.com/2018/08/16/new-wordpress-policy-allows-it-to-shut-down-blogs-of-sandy-hook-deniers/?utm_campaign=Revue%20newsletter&utm_medium=Newsletter&utm_source=The%20Interface)

Following the last few weeks of drama, WordPress is weighing in on the problems with publishers like Alex Jones’ Infowars, which intentionally create conspiracy theories and fake news to gain clicks. WordPress joins the likes of [Facebook, YouTube and others creating outright bans for Jones’ behavior](https://www.vox.com/2018/8/6/17655658/alex-jones-facebook-youtube-conspiracy-theories?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter) from their platforms (he can still use the free, open-source version of WordPress, however).
