### [There’s a reader revenue revolution happening. Will legacy news miss it — again?](https://www.rjionline.org/stories/theres-a-reader-revenue-revolution-happening.-will-legacy-news-miss-it-agai?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

### [A personalised, flexible paywall model is best for your audience](https://www.inma.org/blogs/value-content/post.cfm/personalised-flexible-paywall-model-is-best-for-audience-advertisers?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

INMA report that strict paywalls don’t allow for the nuances required to suit a diverse audience and make advertising less effective.

### [Subscriptions provide a high quality alternative to social media and advertising!](https://www.businessinsider.sg/subscription-platforms-patreon-substack-quality-alternative-to-social-media-2019-8/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

Business Insider explore how memberships and subscriptions can make the internet a better place and replace problematic advertising models.

> *“It’s a healthy, sustainable option for creators and users.”*

### [The NYT is the latest publisher to put up a registration wall](https://whatsnewinpublishing.com/the-new-york-times-is-the-latest-publisher-to-put-up-a-registration-wall/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

A registration wall is when visitors are asked to create a free account to keep reading unmetered articles. The New York Times has recently put up a hard registration wall following some testing they ran last month. Getting all readers to subscribe is a smart tactic that is also being used by the Boston Globe – providing the publishers with more ways to get to know their audience (and more ways to convert them to subscribers later on).

### [‘Journalism Thinking’ doesn’t need a business model. It needs a call to arms](https://medium.com/@christopherwink/journalism-thinking-doesn-t-need-a-business-model-it-needs-a-call-to-arms-c764797b5d99?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

> *The question then isn’t “what is the business model for local journalism” but rather “how do we expand and defend journalism thinking?”*

Christopher Wink shares a different perspective on subscriptions for local journalism. He argues that rather than finding a new business model, local publishers should join forces to form a paradigm shift and rethink journalism.
