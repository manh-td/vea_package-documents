💸 Business models
-----------------

### [1,000 True Fans? Try 100](https://a16z.com/2020/02/06/100-true-fans/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

This article proposes the infamous “1,000 true fans” article by WIRED editor Kevin Kelly, needs to be revisited. It advocates for 100 true fans instead, who are high-paying super fans. The article also offers some recipes for earning $1,000 per fan!

### [What The Athletic’s success teaches us about monetising local news](https://whatsnewinpublishing.com/what-the-athletics-success-teaches-us-about-monetizing-local-news/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

“Since its founding in 2016, the company has gone from city to city and lured the nation’s best sportswriters away from their newspaper jobs in exchange for huge salary bumps and even a cut of the revenue they generate through subscriptions.”

### [Tech In Asia is finally on a path to profitability after a decade of testing business models](https://www.splicemedia.com/tech-in-asia-finds-profitability/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

“Emerging from a series of painful layoffs, the Singapore-based startup finds its way forward.”

### [Why producing less news leads to a boost in subscriptions](https://baekdal.com/analytics/why-producing-less-news-leads-to-a-boost-in-subscriptions/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

Publishers aren’t seeing any negative results when they reduce the number of articles they write and according to this report, it can actually lead to an increase in revenue (34 page report, subscription required).

### [Thrillist diversify revenue with travel packages](https://whatsnewinpublishing.com/thrillist-becomes-the-latest-publisher-to-launch-travel-packages-in-revenue-diversification-bid/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

Food, travel and entertainment site [*Thrillist*](https://www.thrillist.com/travel?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter) are diversifying revenue with travel packages – a strategy which is becoming more common with travel publishers such as [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter) & [Culture Trip](https://theculturetrip.com/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter).

### [Forming habits & recurring behaviour is key for publishers to build a sustainable subscription business](https://www.twipemobile.com/habit-forming-news-products/?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

A key metric for publishers running subscriptions is how active, or engaged, a reader is with the product. The more people come back, and the longer they spend, the more they’re likely to stick around. So how do publishers (re)create habits like reading a newspaper? Get the full report [here](http://eepurl.com/gC6hrb?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter).

### [Maybe information actually doesn’t want to be free](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/02/07/business/media/the-information-jessica-lessin.html?utm_campaign=Publisher%20Weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue%20newsletter)

*“I’ve said this from the beginning,” she said, “and I continue to say this, but you can’t give away what you expect the reader to find valuable.”*Jessica Lessin’s online tech publication costs $399 a year and has no ads. Silicon Valley’s elite is eating it up.
