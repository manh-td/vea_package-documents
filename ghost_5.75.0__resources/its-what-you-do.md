🏔 What mountain are you climbing?
---------------------------------

![](https://ghost.org/resources/content/images/2022/09/Newsletter-icon-2--2-.png)

Forbes recently released a Top 50 Creators list where it ranked influencers from across the social sphere on metrics such as total earnings, audience size, and engagement.

The annual income ranged from $200,000 all the way up to $51 million (*yes, in a single year*). You can see the full list below.

[Top Creators 2022These 49 social media savants—and one dog—are redefining celebrity for our connected age.![](https://i.forbesimg.com/48X48-F.png)ForbesAlexandra Sternlicht![](https://imageio.forbes.com/specials-images/imageserve/6316afbb2af36b290299f8e7/0x0.jpg?format=jpg&crop=1920,1080,x0,y0,safe&width=1200)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alexandrasternlicht/2022/09/06/top-creators-2022/?sh=1bf7e37e67a1&ref=ghost.org)

What's interesting about this is that it was released almost simultaneously with a massive study on creator earnings performed by HubSpot. They found that "the **average salary for content creators** in 2022 is [$44,192 per year](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/content-creator-salary?ref=ghost.org)."

So, what's the takeaway here?

* First, **you're doing better** than you think. If you're on your way to earning your first few hundred or even thousand dollars a month, that's an incredible achievement — and a significant move towards creative and financial freedom.
* Second, **we should be honest** about our odds. Could you make a million dollars a year with your creation...*maybe*. But, could you build a sustainable full-time income around a small audience while making work you're proud of — absolutely! In fact, the odds of the second option are steadily growing.
* Third, **look for good examples** to model your business after. Top 50 creators will have access to opportunities the average person won't. Instead, read stories like the ones our team has published on [Tangle](https://ghost.org/resources/tangle-isaac-saul/), [Maker Stations](https://ghost.org/resources/maker-stations/), and [The Browser](https://ghost.org/resources/the-browser-curation-tips/) to see how regular people can build incredible publications.

🧬 The 5 types of indie newsletter business models
-------------------------------------------------

![](https://ghost.org/resources/content/images/2022/09/Newsletter-image-2--1-.png)

Making great content is the foundation of every successful newsletter. However, that content also needs to be packaged in an attractive way to customers. This *packaging* is the business model.

The team at Inbox Collective published [an excellent guide to five models](https://inboxcollective.com/five-types-of-indie-newsletter-business-models/?ref=ghost.org): **Analyst**, **Curator**, **Expert**, **Reporter**, and **Writer**.

It takes the information found in one of the Ghost Resource articles "[6 types of newsletters you can start today](https://ghost.org/resources/types-of-newsletters/)" and goes one step further by asking four questions of each type:

* What does this newsletter actually do?
* How big is the potential audience?
* How does it grow (*i.e. what growth levers are available for this type of content*)?
* How will it make money?

Most creators never take the time to answer these in depth. Do yourself a favor and get clear on what you're building.

⚓️ The revival of reaction content
----------------------------------

![](https://ghost.org/resources/content/images/2022/09/Newsletter-icon-2--3-.png)

Do you remember when reaction videos were popular on YouTube? Back when the algorithm was much simpler, any creator could post a reaction to a trending topic and garner millions of views.

Those days of easy wins are long past, but the strategy isn't.

[The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/billie-eilish?ref=ghost.org) highlighted one example that provides some interesting insights on growth hacking in today's environment. Here's the setup.

* Johnny Harris, a creator with 2.9 million subscribers, published a video on [the history of Europe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vLpSeMlfZ60&ref=ghost.org).
* The Present Past, a creator with only 77,000 subscribers (*who, btw, is an actual historian*), published a response titled *How Johnny Harris Rewrites History* where they point out the errors made in the original video.
* To date **the response video has performed better**, gaining 200,000+ more views than the original (*1.55 million vs. 1.33 million*).

Here is The Present Past's video in full.

Since the video went live, Johnny Harris gave a very thoughtful response and promised to improve their content going forward — a win for everyone involved. With that in mind, here are a few steps you can use to leverage response content to grow your audience:

1. High-quality responses that are **respectfully** done can constructively engage the original audience and creator.
2. People love drama, and response videos or articles can capitalize on the viral potential of **internet conflict**. (*Just don't make this your entire brand.*)
3. Although influencers are liked, it doesn't always mean they're trusted. By positioning yourself as a niche expert, you can build an **authoritative relationship** with other people's audiences.
